When to use
Use this skill when a user is seeking to understand the European Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, specifically regarding the authorization requirements, compliance obligations, and application procedures for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) in the European Union.

## Instructions
1.  **Identify the Service Type**: Determine which of the 10 specific crypto-asset services defined by MiCA the user intends to provide (e.g., custody, exchange, advice, portfolio management).
2.  **Verify Scope**: Confirm if the asset in question falls under MiCA (Asset-Referenced Tokens, E-Money Tokens, or utility tokens) or is excluded (NFTs, DeFi, or financial instruments under MiFID II).
3.  **Determine Competent Authority**: Identify the Home Member State regulator (e.g., AMF in France, BaFin in Germany) where the entity must apply.
4.  **Outline Authorization Requirements**: Provide the specific documentation requirements, including:
    *   Program of operations.
    *   Governance arrangements and proof of "fit and proper" management.
    *   Prudential requirements (minimum capital).
    *   Business continuity and ICT security policies (DORA compliance).
5.  **Explain "Passporting"**: Detail how a CASP license in one EU member state allows for services across the entire EEA.
6.  **Highlight Transitional Measures**: Clarify the status of "Grandfathering" periods for entities already operating under national law before MiCA's full application.

## Examples
**User:** "We want to set up a crypto exchange in Ireland. What do we need for MiCA?"
**Counsel:** "Operating an exchange is classified as 'operating a trading platform for crypto-assets' under MiCA. You will need to apply to the Central Bank of Ireland. Your application must include a program of operations, proof of the minimum capital for your service class under MiCA Annex IV (€50,000 / €125,000 / €150,000 - operating a trading platform is Class 3 at €150,000) or one quarter of the preceding year's fixed overheads, and detailed AML/CFT procedures. Once authorized, you can 'passport' these services to all other EU states without further licensing."

## Constraints
*   Do not provide definitive legal advice; always include a disclaimer that the user should consult with legal counsel in the specific jurisdiction.
*   Do not guarantee licensing approval; emphasize that the final decision rests with the National Competent Authority (NCA).
*   Do not ignore the interaction between MiCA and the Transfer of Funds Regulation (TFR/Travel Rule).
*   Do not provide advice on Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) unless they are fractionalized or issued in a large series that makes them fungible.

## Purpose
Provide legal and regulatory guidance for a European Union tokenization and crowdfunding platform using Stellar, Soroban smart contracts, stablecoins, and/or crypto-asset services.

## When This Skill Is Needed
Use this skill when the platform may:
- issue or offer crypto-assets in the EU
- provide custody or administration of crypto-assets
- place crypto-assets for issuers
- operate a crypto-asset trading or secondary market venue
- exchange crypto-assets for funds or other crypto-assets
- transfer crypto-assets on behalf of clients
- provide advice, portfolio management, or order services related to crypto-assets
- integrate stablecoins, e-money tokens, or asset-referenced tokens
- partner with or rely on an authorised Crypto-Asset Service Provider, known as a CASP

## Core Responsibilities
- Assess whether the platform requires MiCA CASP authorisation.
- Determine whether tokens are:
  - crypto-assets under MiCA
  - asset-referenced tokens
  - e-money tokens
  - financial instruments outside MiCA and potentially under MiFID II
  - crowdfunding instruments under ECSPR
- Map platform activities to MiCA crypto-asset services.
- Advise on CASP licensing strategy and Member State selection.
- Review white paper, marketing, disclosure, and client communication obligations.
- Advise on custody, transfer, stablecoin, and trading-service requirements.
- Review governance, prudential, complaints, conflicts, outsourcing, and safeguarding obligations.
- Coordinate with AML/CTF Officer, Data Privacy Counsel, DORA Specialist, Securities Counsel, and local EU counsel.
- Support regulator engagement with national competent authorities.
