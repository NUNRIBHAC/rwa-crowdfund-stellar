# Criteria for comparing investment platforms

Date: November 23, 2026

## Purpose and limits

Bring together the criteria used by comparison sites and the eight specified platforms to guide a future Blockallisto checklist. A complete platform should answer every applicable criterion, even if the answer is "not offered" or "not applicable". It does not need to offer every competitor's products.

This document does not include a "present / partial / missing" column: the Blockallisto code audit is pending. No product changes were made. The list is neither legal validation nor an approved implementation specification.

## The 13 criteria groups

### 1. Regulation and identity
- Operating entity, identification and group structure.
- Licence type and scope, regulator, number, date and countries covered.
- Match between the licensed entity and the entity providing the service.
- Links to official registers and information about regulator warnings.
- Presence or absence of compensation or guarantee schemes, without confusing these with protection against investment losses.

Source: earlier CrowdIndex and p2pinvestitor research; regime distinctions also in [S]. Licence claims need confirmation in the official register before use as a compliance badge.

### 2. Investor protection
- Investor classification and eligibility.
- Knowledge test, simulation of the ability to bear losses and applicable warnings/consents.
- Key investment information sheet and pre-contractual documentation.
- Applicable reflection period and process for withdrawing an intention to invest.
- Risk of total loss, illiquidity, dilution and lack of dividends.

Source: earlier research and Crowdcube's own warnings [Q]. Legal thresholds and requirements were not audited again in this supplement.

### 3. Track record and performance
- Years operating, capital financed and number of investors/projects.
- Defaults, arrears, recoveries, losses and recovery times.
- Realised net return versus advertised return.
- History of withdrawal delays.
- Dates, population and methodology of statistics; separate samples from actual activity.

Source: earlier research; reporting and recovery in [T].

### 4. Owners, conflicts and concentration
- Shareholders, team, originators and project owners.
- Related parties and relationships between operator, financier and project.
- Concentration by originator, country, sector and project.
- Co-investment and own capital at risk, with explicit conditions.

Source: earlier research; originators and economic participation presented in [L].

### 5. Collateral, guarantees and recovery
- Existence, nature and valuation of collateral; LTV where applicable.
- Priority and enforcement of security, guarantor and its financial capacity.
- Buyback: responsible party, conditions and risk of that party itself defaulting.
- Recovery process and information made available to the investor.
- If a provision fund exists, its nature, size and limitations, as an analysis criterion, not a proposal to create one.

Source: earlier research; [T,L]. A guarantee does not mean capital is safe.

### 6. Liquidity, costs and terms
- Minimum investment, term, maturity and payment schedule.
- Manual, automated or managed investment and diversification/reinvestment rules.
- Distinguish private transfers, sale windows, secondary markets, conditional early redemption and maturity.
- Conditions, costs and absence of any guarantee of finding a buyer.
- Investor fees: entry, withdrawal, inactivity, maintenance, recovery, sale, success and liquidity.
- Project owner fees: application/listing, success, processing, maintenance and nominee, including treatment of taxes on fees.
- Separate interest, operating income/rent and sale capital gains; state assumptions and uncertainty.

Source: earlier research; new detail in [G,F,I,N,K,T,L]. Automation, management and exit mechanisms require validation of the regulatory framework before implementation.

### 7. Transparency and public information
- Audited accounts, timely publication and auditor qualifications.
- Portfolio statistics, recoveries and material management changes.
- Offer status: open, funded, in progress, overdue, in recovery or closed.
- Data source and date; distinguish unpublished information from information unavailable without an account.
- Number of genuinely open offers and history, without presenting placeholders as real data.

Source: earlier research; expansion in [S,T,E].

### 8. Operations, accessibility and continuity
- Languages, customer support, contact channels and mobile/app presence.
- Frequency of new offers and operational specialisation.
- Deposit and withdrawal methods, times and entity holding/processing funds.
- Portfolio interface, filters, documents and transactions.
- Press and independent reviews, distinguished from testimonials selected by the operator.
- Current operator status and continuity of legacy projects if new fundraising stops.

Source: earlier research; [T,L,R,K,C].

### 9. Red flags
- Guaranteed-return promises without explanation of risks.
- Sales pressure, artificial urgency and misleading countdowns.
- Unreachable contacts, hidden costs and contradictory information.
- Confusion between regulation, deposit protection and protection against losses.

Source: earlier research. These are control criteria, not findings about Blockallisto.

### 10. Catalogue and suitability, new
- Legal instrument and specific economic rights.
- Sector/asset, country, company stage and project stage.
- Eligible profile and geographical or access restrictions.
- Clear separation between a commercial product and authorisation to provide it.

Source: [R,S,E].

### 11. Equity investor rights, new
- Direct ownership or ownership through a nominee and identification of legal/beneficial holder.
- Share class, voting and collective decision rules.
- Pre-emption in new rounds, follow-on and dilution risk.
- Dividends, documentation, certificates and handling of a company sale.
- Transfer conditions and differences between offers.

Source: [N,R]. Do not assume every investment gives the same rights or copy a UK structure into Portugal.

### 12. Support for fundraising companies, new
- Size and profile of the investor community.
- Private/public campaigns and access to the platform's investor base.
- Marketing, public relations, CRM and community tools.
- Legal/regulatory support, checks and process times.
- Company selection criteria and evidence required for offer claims.

Source: [H,F,D]. Shark Ponds provides editorial criteria, mainly from the US; its statistics were not treated as certified facts.

### 13. Relationships and post-investment support, new
- Questions to project owners and information requests.
- Videos, presentations, business plans and forecasts, with access rules.
- Company updates, portfolio and communications about new rounds.
- Administration of dividends, votes, sales and other investment events.

Source: [R,G,N,I].

## Debitum / ECSPR exclusion

Within this research scope, Debitum counts only insofar as a criterion is relevant to ECSPR. Its site states that it operates as an investment firm under MiFID II [B]. Notes/ABS under that framework, its own regulatory structure and the Flow product are excluded from this research's build candidates.

This is not a legal finding that every instrument with that name is prohibited. It means that compatibility with the intended Blockallisto licence and ECSPR model has not been demonstrated. Generic criteria also supported by other operators, such as minimums, terms, risks, costs, collateral and exit conditions, remain.

UK tax benefits, FCA rules, US SEC regimes, nominee structures or trading/management services must not be imported automatically into Portugal. Every instrument and service needs validation of its authorised scope.

## Important corrections

- **Seedrs is Republic Europe.** The rebrand was announced for July 10, 2024 [J].
- **Crowdestate is no longer presented by its own site as an active marketplace.** It states that it ended its own crowdfunding operation and returned that licence. Its focus shifted to infrastructure and white-label services; access to and servicing of legacy projects continue under the conditions stated by the operator [C].
- **Comparison-site prices may be outdated.** Good Money Guide still describes no listing fee at Crowdcube, while Crowdcube's own help publishes Focus/Full Access fees [G,F]. The comparison site was used to extract criteria, not establish current prices.

### New checklist details, integrated into the existing groups

- **Groups 2 and 8:** KYC for individuals, companies and representatives; proof of bank account ownership and source of funds; suspension, unblocking, closure and data retention.
- **Groups 5 and 8:** service continuity if the operator fails, replacement administrator and segregation of uninvested funds. EstateGuru describes these mechanisms; this does not guarantee the investment.
- **Group 6:** term extension rules, effects on interest and activation of buyback; difference between stopping future investments and reversing existing ones.
- **Groups 2, 6 and 13:** cancellation, refunds for cancelled campaigns or unresolved KYC, collection timing, confirmation and document delivery. The timing described by Crowdcube must not be imported as a Portuguese ECSPR rule.
- **Group 8:** formal complaints and escalation procedure, beyond support contacts.
- **Groups 10 and 11:** credit priority/subordination; if a token exists, what it represents and wallet, key and blockchain risks. This is a disclosure criterion, not a proposal to issue every instrument described by Exporo.
- **Group 13:** statements, tax certificates and ownership documents, respecting jurisdiction and product. Crowdcube describes documents in the portfolio. Lendermarket's tax-report question appeared without an answer in the extraction, so that feature was not confirmed.

### Additional contradictions and caveats

- Lendermarket's homepage marks a flexible option as future, while its FAQ already describes Auto Invest FLEX. General availability without an account was not confirmed.
- Crowdestate retains an April 2025 closure/liquidation announcement and a current homepage focused on infrastructure. No conclusion is drawn that liquidation has been completed.
- Brickstarter's terms describe the balance as a user's claim against the company, with funds held in company accounts. Do not treat this as a segregation/custody example to copy.
- The Exporo page combines different instruments and regimes. A feature appearing on the site does not prove it falls within ECSPR.
- Debitum was consulted only for identity and regulatory scope; MiFID II/Flow products were not added to the candidates.
- Private rules, portfolios, individual contracts, investment-specific documents and account operations remain untested. There was no login. Not every dynamic question returned an answer.

These are **89 name records**, not 89 independent, active or ECSPR operators. - **Direct, subject to validation:** European crowdfunding described as ECSPR/ECSP; separate products of other entities/regimes.
- **Generic:** interface, information or operations may inspire criteria; do not import products or regulatory frameworks.
- **Historical:** study continuity and disclosure, not a new active offer.
- **Excluded:** insufficient identity/content or outside the limits imposed for Debitum.

### New criteria found in the wider reading

The 13 groups remain; these details fit within them:

- **Groups 7 and 10, impact/ESG:** environmental and social metrics, units, methodology, period and distinction between estimates and outcomes. Goparity shows avoided CO2 and impact reporting; Ener2Crowd describes environmental/social impact. A criterion for avoiding vague claims, not independent certification of the metrics. Sources: https://goparity.com/ and https://www.ener2crowd.com/en/home .
- **Groups 5, 6 and 13, phased funding:** conditions and milestones for releasing tranches, who verifies them and communication of deviations. Recrowd describes disbursements linked to project progress; the platform's current regulatory status remains unresolved in this report. Source: https://www.recrowd.com/ .
- **Groups 11 and 12, capital structure management:** cap table, investor aggregation, co-investment, lead investor role and syndicate costs/conflicts. SeedBlink and Spreds show these dimensions. This is not a proposal to create a fund, SPV, nominee or management service without validation. Sources: https://seedblink.com/ and https://www.spreds.com/en .
- **Groups 8 and 12, fundraising integrations:** API, CRM tools and digital subscription/shareholder management. Wefunder shows API and fundraising services; DealMaker shows subscriptions, cap table and communications. Copying an operational criterion does not import US authorisation or authorise integration or data sharing. Sources: https://wefunder.com/ and https://www.dealmaker.tech/ .
- **Groups 1 and 10, separation by entity/regime:** one group may provide crowdfunding, funds, financial instruments and other services. The checklist should identify which entity provides which service and under what conditions, not label the whole group with one badge.
- **Groups 8 and 13, migration/run-off:** destination of contracts, money, history and documents; who continues collecting and providing support. Re-Lender and October demonstrate the usefulness of this criterion. Sources: https://www.relender.eu/continuita-operativa/ and https://october.eu/lenders/migration-faq/ .
