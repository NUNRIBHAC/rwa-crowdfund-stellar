# Risk Disclosure

**Last updated:** [date to be set on publication]

## Read this first: today's demo involves no real money

Blockallisto is currently a **prototype on the Stellar testnet**. All contributions use test tokens with **no monetary value**. You cannot lose real money on this demo, and nothing on it is a real investment opportunity.

The warnings below explain the risks that **would** apply to the kind of investing Blockallisto demonstrates - crowdfunding into real-world assets through crypto-asset infrastructure. They are published now so that the risk conversation is part of the product from day one.

## General warning

Investing in crowdfunding projects - whether in real-world assets such as property or businesses, or through crypto-assets and tokens - is **high risk**. You could lose **all of the money you invest**. Such investments are typically **not covered** by deposit-guarantee schemes or investor-compensation schemes.

**Never invest money you cannot afford to lose.** For a sense of scale, the reference platforms in our research corpus carry concrete diversification guidance: CONDA's risk-warnings page advises investing no more than about 10% of your freely available funds in this asset class, and the standard Austrian crowdfunding investor sheet (AltFG regime, observed on dagobertinvest) warns against investing more than 10% of net assets. This is the observed risk-communication practice of established platforms, cited as a reference point - it is not a rule of law that applies to this demo, and not a guarantee that any percentage is safe for your situation. Spreading a smaller amount across several projects reduces, but never removes, the risk of total loss.

## Specific risks

- **Capital loss.** The project you back may fail, underperform or become worthless. Issuers can and do become insolvent after successful fundraises. Returns are never guaranteed.
- **Illiquidity.** Crowdfunded and tokenized positions can be hard or impossible to sell. There may be no secondary market, transfers can require the issuer's or platform's consent and a verified recipient, and you may have to hold until the project ends - or longer. (On the current prototype no transferable token exists at all.)
- **Subordination.** Subordinated or "junior" instruments rank behind other creditors; with qualified subordination you may receive nothing while the issuer is over-indebted, and interest or repayment claims can be suspended entirely.
- **Term and extension risk.** Loans and bonds may be extended beyond their stated term under their contracts, delaying repayment.
- **Valuation risk.** Real-world assets are hard to value; stated valuations may be wrong or stale. (On this demo, all valuations and issuer identities are mock data.)
- **Smart-contract risk.** Code can contain bugs or be exploited, and transactions on a blockchain are generally irreversible.
- **Network risk.** Blockchain networks can suffer congestion, outages, forks or protocol changes outside anyone's control.
- **Key and wallet risk.** If you lose your wallet's secret key or recovery phrase, or someone steals them, your funds are gone and no one can restore them.
- **Regulatory risk.** Rules for crowdfunding (ECSPR in the EU) and crypto-assets (MiCA) are evolving. A structure that is possible today may change, and offerings may be restricted where you live.
- **Volatility and currency risk.** Crypto-asset prices can move sharply; stablecoins can de-peg; exchange rates move.
- **Counterparty and fraud risk.** Issuers may mismanage funds or misrepresent their projects. Platform checks reduce but cannot eliminate this.

## About future investor protections

EU crowdfunding regulation (the ECSPR, Regulation (EU) 2020/1503) requires authorised platforms to offer investor categorisation, an entry knowledge test, a simulation of the ability to bear loss, and a pre-contractual reflection period of four calendar days for non-sophisticated investors before an offer becomes binding; the reference platforms in our research corpus implement these under their own authorisations. Blockallisto's demo has none of these mechanisms, because no real investment is possible. If a regulated version ever launches, those protections - and their limits - will be described in its own legally reviewed documents. Protections like reflection periods let you withdraw an offer; they do not reduce the underlying investment risk.

## No advice

Nothing on this Service is investment, legal or tax advice, or a recommendation to buy or sell anything. Past performance - real or simulated - does not predict future results. Consider independent professional advice, and diversify: do not concentrate your savings in any single project or asset type.
